The task

One question, one corpus, one corrected number you can defend

  • The director's question from your brief, in one sentence
  • The corpus prepared and redacted, with its miss rate
  • A gold set with agreement, and a model scored on it
  • Fields extracted and validated per field
  • The corrected estimate with its interval
  • The reliability memo and the version record

Corpora

Nadwa libraries or Raafid social support (synthetic, in labs/data/capstone/), or a public corpus the trainer approved. Extraction uses the course's 500 inspection reports unless your corpus has its own fields.

Every item maps to a lab: Day 1 for preparation, Day 2 for agreement and classifiers, Day 3 for the memo, Day 4 for extraction and the corrected rate. The capstone corpora have no fields to extract, so the extraction criterion is scored on the inspection reports or on a reference set of at least 50 documents the participant built from their own corpus.

The rubric is 60% of the grade, the live defence 20%, the AI-off checks 20%. Passing: 70% overall and at least 60% on both the rubric and the supervised check. Walk through the next slide before anyone rehearses.
What full marks look like

Each criterion has one thing that separates a pass from full marks

CriterionFull marks
Preparation and redactionA near-duplicate rule justified by a threshold table; a redaction miss rate with its interval
Gold set and agreementKappa by group, and the disagreements read and explained
Models and extractionTwo methods compared on the same gold set, paired; precision and recall per field
Reliability memoCriteria set before the results; separate answers for per-document and aggregate use
ReproducibilityLock file, prompts, saved outputs and a version record for every label set
These are the examples to give when someone asks what "good" means. The most common gap in a first draft is a corrected estimate with no interval, or an interval without saying where its uncertainty comes from.
