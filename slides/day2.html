Correcting for chance

Kappa is the agreement beyond chance, as a share of what was possible beyond chance

(po − pe) / (1 − pe)
pe: the sum over categories of each coder's share in it, multiplied
Reference pairValue
Observed agreement po0.750
Chance agreement pe0.144
Cohen's kappa0.708

Work p_e out loud for two categories so the formula is concrete. With eight fairly balanced categories chance agreement is low, so kappa is close to agreement. The next slide is the case where it is not. Sources: scikit-learn, cohen_kappa_score.

What agreement cannot show

Two coders can agree and both be wrong in the same way

  • Kappa measures consistency, not correctness
  • Coders trained together share their blind spots
  • SDAIA's AI Bias Reference Guide names this labelling bias: labels shaped by the annotators' own interpretations

What the guide recommends

Clear, objective criteria; annotators of diverse backgrounds; several annotators per item with disagreements resolved by consensus or expert review.

Read from the guide's entry on labelling bias, in our own words. At Sada this is why the coders include speakers of Gulf dialect: a coder who reads Gulf text slowly makes systematic errors that a second coder of the same background repeats. Sources: AI Bias Reference Guide, first edition (SDAIA, May 2026).

Pair A, since they agree more often
Pair B labels water and sewage more correctly
They cannot be compared at all
Pair B agrees more beyond chance; Pair A's 94% is mostly agreement on "no"
Why. With a rare category most agreement is on the negative and would happen by chance; kappa removes it.

Answer: the fourth option. The second option is the subtle trap: kappa is reliability, not validity.

Features

Character n-grams let different forms of one Arabic word share evidence

الحديقهحديقديقهحديقتناحديقديقت
  • The article ال and suffixes such as نا change the word but keep most of its pieces
  • char_wb: n-grams inside word boundaries, 2 to 5 characters
  • Normalisation and the dialect map from Day 1 run first, so spelling variants collapse

The chips show the shared four-character pieces of "the garden" and "our garden". A word-level model sees two unrelated words. The stretch of Lab 2.2 tests this: in the course's run word n-grams lost most on Modern Standard Arabic.

Weights

TF-IDF weights a piece by how often it appears here and how rare it is elsewhere

vec = TfidfVectorizer(analyzer="char_wb", ngram_range=(2, 5),
                      min_df=2, sublinear_tf=True)
X_pool = vec.fit_transform(prep(pool.text))   # fit on the pool only
X_gold = vec.transform(prep(gold.text))
model = LogisticRegression(max_iter=2000, C=5).fit(X_pool, pool.routing_label)

8,566 features; trained and scored in 6.4 seconds on a laptop.

Point at "fit on the pool only": fitting the vectoriser on the gold set lets the test vocabulary into training. The 6.4 seconds is from the course's run on an ordinary laptop processor. Sources: scikit-learn, TfidfVectorizer.

The training labels

The pool's labels are the intake team's routing, fast and unchecked

  • 3,000 redacted complaints, each routed once by intake
  • Some routing is wrong, and not at random: permits and staff conduct trade places, so do waste and parks
  • A model trained on them learns the habits too

The rule

Train on the noisy labels, score on the gold set. Never score on the labels you trained on.

The generator routes about 12% of pool complaints to the wrong category, mostly to a neighbouring one; that is the trainer's answer key, not something participants can see. Scattered errors average out; systematic ones are learnt. The gold set is how you find out which.

Reading an interval

The interval for English is twice as wide because it rests on 66 complaints

  • Bootstrap: resample the gold complaints with replacement, recompute, repeat 2,000 times
  • The middle 95% of the results is the interval
  • Width shrinks with the square root of n: four times the complaints halves it
What goes wrong. "English is 0.924 and Arabic 0.921, so they are equal." The intervals say the gold set cannot tell.

Ask: how many English gold complaints would make the English interval as narrow as the overall one? About 300. That is a gold-set design question for the capstone.

Embeddings

An embedding places texts so that similar meanings sit close together

  • A model reads the text and returns a fixed-length vector
  • Close vectors: complaints about the same thing, even in different words or languages
  • A simple classifier on top learns the categories from a few examples each

What it loses

Which words carried the decision. A wrong label is harder to explain than with n-gram weights.

Multilingual embedding models put an Arabic and an English complaint about a broken street light near each other; character n-grams cannot. Whether that helps depends on whether the training data already covers each language, which at Sada it does. Sources: Sentence Transformers documentation.

Checkpoint

How do you read +0.037 with an interval of −0.003 to +0.073?

The paired difference in gold accuracy, sparse minus embedding, is +0.037 with a 95% interval from −0.003 to +0.073. Which sentence belongs in the report?

The two classifiers perform the same
The sparse classifier is probably better by up to seven points, but a difference of zero cannot be ruled out
The sparse classifier is 3.7 points better
The embedding classifier may be the better one, since the interval is negative
Why. The interval states the range of differences the gold set allows: mostly in favour of the sparse model, zero just inside.

Answer: the second option. Spend a minute on the first option: "same performance" is the most common misreading in written reports.

Day 2 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Agreement beyond chance

Kappa, by category, with the rare categories read carefully. It measures consistency, not truth.

Score on gold

Train on the labels you have; score on the labels people checked, with intervals by language.

Pair the comparison

Same documents, paired differences, an interval, then cost and risk.

Ask which classifier each table would deploy tomorrow, and what evidence would change their mind.
