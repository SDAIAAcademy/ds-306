# Follow-up task: audit one labelling process

A tracked task after the course. You audit one process at your own organisation, or in a published source, that
turns text into labels or counts, using the checks of this course. It takes about three hours and is due four weeks
after the course.

## What to choose

Any process where people or a model put text into categories and someone uses the counts: complaint routing, ticket
categories, survey comments coded by theme, a model that tags documents, a published report built on coded text.
Work only with text you are allowed to use. Do not copy personal data into the audit: describe, count and quote
redacted examples only. If the text is classified above Public, do not send it to a hosted model; ask your data
owner which tools are permitted.

## The checklist

Answer each question in one to three sentences, with a number wherever you can produce one.

1. **The question and the decision.** Which decision do the labels or counts feed? Who reads them?
2. **The unit and the codebook.** What is one document? Is there a written codebook with a rule for documents that
   name two problems? When did it last change?
3. **Preparation.** Are copies and resubmissions removed? Is an organised campaign counted as one issue or many?
   Is personal data removed before anyone else, or any model, sees the text?
4. **The gold set.** Is there a sample labelled carefully by people? How was it drawn? How large is it?
5. **Agreement.** Have two people labelled the same documents? What is their agreement, corrected for chance?
   If nobody has measured it, label 30 documents twice yourself (with a colleague) and report kappa.
6. **The labeller.** Person, rule or model? If a model: which one, which prompt, which settings, and is the version
   recorded with every label?
7. **Stability and groups.** Has anyone run the model twice, or compared its accuracy by language, dialect, channel
   or region?
8. **Downstream.** Are the published counts corrected for the labeller's measured errors? Do they carry an interval?
9. **The biggest risk.** Which group, place or category is most likely to be under- or over-counted, and why?
10. **Your recommendation.** In three sentences: what the process can be trusted for, what it cannot, and the one
    change that would most improve it.

## What to submit

A Markdown file of at most 1,000 words with the answers to the ten questions, the agreement figure from question 5
and your recommendation. Add it to your capstone repository as `followup.md`, or submit it as your trainer directs.

## How it is read

The trainer reads for the use of the course's checks, not for a clean result. An audit that finds a process
without a gold set, or with low agreement, is a good audit when it says so with evidence.
